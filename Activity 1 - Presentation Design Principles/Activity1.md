## Overview

This activity allowed me to explore the basic principles that make a presentation clear, organized, and easy to understand. It focused on how the arrangement of text, images, colors, and other elements can affect the way information is presented.

## Concept

For this activity, I aimed to create a presentation that was simple but still visually interesting. I wanted the information to remain the main focus instead of using too many decorative elements. The overall design was planned to make the content easier to follow while maintaining a clean appearance.

## Design Principles Applied

### Visual Hierarchy

I used different font sizes, placements, and levels of emphasis to show which information was more important. This helped direct attention to the main points before the supporting details.

### Alignment

I arranged the text and visual elements properly so that the slides would have a more organized and balanced appearance. Proper alignment also helped prevent the presentation from looking messy.

### Consistency

I kept the colors, fonts, spacing, and general style similar throughout the presentation. This made the slides look more connected and gave the entire presentation a uniform appearance.

### Simplicity

I avoided adding elements that were not necessary for the message. By keeping the design simple, I was able to make the content easier to read without making the slides feel too crowded.

## Development Process

I first organized the information that needed to be included and planned how each part would be presented. After that, I worked on the layout and applied the design principles to the slides. I reviewed the presentation several times and adjusted the placement, spacing, text, and visual elements to improve its overall appearance.

## What I Learned

This activity taught me that creating a good presentation involves more than putting information on slides. The way the content is arranged and presented can greatly affect how easily the audience understands the message. I also learned that simple and organized designs can be more effective than adding too many visual elements.
